# S51. The first moment behind (L³) is a twisted G₂ Weyl-group multiple Dirichlet series (23:25Z)

## 1. The object
From S50, the T-specific input is the Gauss-weighted first moment. For a twist h₀ it is

  X(h₀; Q, Z) = Σ_m V(N(m)/Q) R̄(m) g̃(χ̄_m) ψ_m(h₀),   with R(m) = Σ_c T(c)χ̄_c(m)W(N(c)/Z) and T(c) = α(c)g̃(χ̄_c).

Its generating function is the double Dirichlet series (after class splitting mod 16; |·| unchanged by conjugation)

  Z_{h₀}(s,w) = Σ_{c,d} [α(c) g̃(χ̄_c)] · [g̃(h₀; χ_d)] · χ̄_d(c) · N(c)^{−s} N(d)^{−w}.

The c-node carries Gauss sums of χ̄ (power 3) and the Hecke character α (ℓ = 1). The d-node carries Gauss sums of χ (power 1), untwisted.

## 2. The two generating functional equations (PROVED from DDHL (4.28); index bookkeeping checked)
Use coordinates x = s − ½, y = w − ½.
* **c-node (d fixed).** Σ_c α(c)g̃(χ̄_c)χ̄_d(c)N(c)^{−s} is an α-twisted Kubota series of χ̄-orientation at index d³, since χ̄_c(d) = χ_c(d³).
  Its FE s ↦ 1−s carries N(d³)^{½−s}, so σ_c: (x, y) ↦ (−x, y + 3x).
  The factor (ν/|ν|)^ℓ moves an α^{±3}-twist onto the d-node.
* **d-node (c fixed).** Σ_d g̃(h₀;χ_d)χ̄_d(c)N(d)^{−w} = ψ(h₀c, w), the untwisted Kubota series at index h₀c.
  Its FE w ↦ 1−w carries N(h₀c)^{½−w}, so σ_d: (x, y) ↦ (x + y, −y).
* **The group.** σ_cσ_d = [[−1,−1],[3,2]] has trace 1 and determinant 1, so it has order 6. ⟨σ_c, σ_d⟩ is dihedral of order 12, i.e. W(G₂).
  * The c-reflection moves the d-coordinate by 3x, so ⟨α_c^∨, α_d⟩ = −3 and ⟨α_d^∨, α_c⟩ = −1: c is the SHORT root and d the LONG root
    (corrected in §8; an earlier draft of this line had them swapped).
  * In Chinta–Gunnells' normalisation with the residue symbol ε³ (§8), the Gauss-sum powers are 3‖α‖²: short root 3 (χ̄), long root 9 ≡ 1 (χ).
  * So the c-node (short root) has Gauss sums of χ³ = χ̄, the d-node (long root) has Gauss sums of χ, and the coupling exponent is
    3·2⟨α_short, α_long⟩ = 3·(−3) = −9 ≡ −1, i.e. χ̄_d(c). All three match.
  * This corrects S50 Step 6/8: "opposite orientation ⇒ not a WMDS" holds for equal-length roots (A2) but not for G₂, where the two nodes
    must have different Q.
* **Consistency with S44.** The (L³) second moment Σ_m ψ(m³,s₁)conj ψ(m³,s̄₂)N(m)^{−w} has nodes c, m, c′.
  * The m-sum is a Hecke L-function of conductor cc′, which shifts both s_i by (w−½).
  * The c- and c′-FEs shift w by 3(s_i−½).
  * Coxeter diagram: c —6— m —6— c′, with c and c′ commuting. That is the hyperbolic triangle group (2,6,6), exactly S44's (subagent F) result.
  * So the Cauchy route of S50 trades the hyperbolic (2,6,6) second moment for a finite G₂ first moment, at the price of the Z² floor.

## 3. What G₂ theory would give (HEURISTIC identification; continuation STANDARD in principle)
Expected: Z_{h₀} is, up to finite p-part corrections at non-coprime (c,d), class characters mod 16 and the squarefree corrections
(dFDH (9.6)-type ∆* factors), the Whittaker coefficient of a Borel Eisenstein series on the 4-fold BD cover of G₂ over Q(i).
That series is induced from a torus character with ∞-type α along the coroot α_c^∨ of the short root c.
* Continuation and the W(G₂) functional equations follow from Langlands–Moeglin–Waldspurger theory for finite central covers.
  Alternatively, use Bochner's tube theorem with the finite group, as in Bump–Friedberg–Hoffstein, once the twist data are closed under the FEs.
  Since |W| = 12, the orbit of (ℓ_c, ℓ_d) twist data is finite.
* **Polar divisors.** A pole along a positive root β needs the torus character to be unramified at ∞ on β^∨. Writing positive coroots
  in simple coroots, only α_d^∨ has zero α_c^∨-coefficient. So the only polar hyperplane is the d-root one, y = 1/4: the theta pole,
  w = 3/4, with residue τ₄(h₀c).
* **The residue line.** The reflection fixing y = 1/4 is −σ_d (here −I ∈ W(G₂)): (x,y) ↦ (−x−y, y). On y = 1/4 it fixes x = −1/8.
  So r(s) = Σ_c T(c)τ₄(h₀c)N(c)^{−s} has an FE s ↔ 3/4 − s, centred at s = 3/8: exactly the square-root exponent of (P4).
* **Bounds.** Convexity (Phragmén–Lindelöf on tubes) then gives
  * 𝒞(h₀) = Σ_c T(c)W τ₄(h₀c) ≪ Z^{3/8+ε}N(h₀)^{B};
  * X(h₀) ≪ Q^{3/4}Z^{3/8+ε}N(h₀)^{B} + (QZ)^{1/2+ε}N(h₀)^{B′}.
* **Payoff (S50 bookkeeping).** With B ≤ 7/8 and B′ ≤ 1, Off_T ≤ Z^{3}, so the main box has S ≪ Z^{2+ε}, i.e. the (L³) sum ≪ Q^{3/2+ε}.
  The other boxes and the m₄-slices need the same input with extra twists (n, n′, coprimality to j or m₄): STANDARD extension.

## 4. Reflection walk (PROVED, exponent algebra), independent of the full G₂ theory
On length exponents (q, z) = log_Z(m-length, c-length): σ_m: (q, z) ↦ (z − q + log h₀, z) and σ_c: (q, z) ↦ (q, 3q − z).
* From (2/3, 1): σ_m gives (1/3, 1) plus the theta term, then σ_c gives c′-length ≤ N(h₀)³.
* After two reflections the remainder is Σ_{m′≲h₀Z^{1/3}}Σ_{c′≲h₀³} of unit-size coefficients times (Q/√(h₀Z))(Z/N(m′)^{3/2}).
  * Trivially this is ≪ Z·N(h₀)²; with square-root cancellation it is ≍ (QZ)^{1/2}.
  * For h₀ = 1, c′ = O(1). The m′-sum is then an ℓ = ±3 Kubota series: the α-twist moved by σ_c, so it is entire. A smooth sum of length
    Z^{1/3} at bounded conductor is negligible.
* So for small h₀, X(h₀) = Q^{3/4}V̂(3/4)𝒞(h₀) + O(Z^{−A}). Everything reduces to 𝒞, the residue series r(s).

## 6. Classification by index exponent (PROVED, linear algebra; explains the difficulty of (L³))
For the index family ν = m^r, the c-node FE shifts y by r·x, and the m-node FE (Kubota series in m, or a Hecke L-function of conductor cc′)
shifts each x by y.
* **First moments** Σ_m g̃(·_m)ψ(m^r, s)N(m)^{−w} have a finite dihedral FE group:
  r = 1: A2 (order 6); r = 2: B2 (order 8); r = 3: G2 (order 12); r = 4: no finite order.
* **Second moments** Σ_m ψ(m^r)·conj ψ(m^r) are 3-node objects c—k—m—k—c′, with Coxeter group (2,k,k) and k = 3, 4, 6 for r = 1, 2, 3:
  * r = 1: A3, finite. The all-index family, a large-sieve problem.
  * r = 2: (2,4,4), affine. The squares family; David–Dunn–Hamieh–Lin prove A^{3/2} and expect A^{1+ε} "with additional work".
  * r = 3: (2,6,6), hyperbolic. This is (L³).
* **Correction to S44.** It identified Σ_m g̃(χ̄_m)ψψ as A3 by using BBF's index-m series. Our T-family R(m) = ψ_ᾱ^{BBF}(m̄³) has index m³,
  so that object is (2,6,6) like (L³). Only the first moment is finite (G₂).
* **The S50/S51 strategy in one line.** Cauchy–Schwarz turns the hyperbolic (2,6,6) second moment into the finite G₂ first moment.
  The cost is the Z² floor, i.e. Q^{3/2}. Going below Q^{3/2} needs a genuinely hyperbolic-type input. No multiple-Dirichlet-series
  technique exists for that; it is the analogue of the sixth moment of quadratic L-functions.

## 8. Literature check: Chinta–Gunnells, "Constructing Weyl group multiple Dirichlet series" (JAMS 2010; lit/cg/cg.txt, arXiv 0803.0691)
* **Their Theorem 1.1.** For every reduced root system Φ (G₂ included), every n ≥ 1 and every twisting parameter m, the series
  Z(s; m, Ψ; Φ, n) has analytic continuation to Cʳ and a group of functional equations isomorphic to W(Φ).
  * Hypothesis: F ⊇ μ_{2n}.
  * Ψ ranges over locally constant S-adic class functions M(Ω,Φ).
* **Their coefficients (4.3)–(4.4).** Node i carries Gauss sums g(·, c_i; ε^{‖α_i‖²}). Adjacent nodes are coupled by (c_i/c_j)^{2⟨α_i,α_j⟩}.
  For G₂ (‖short‖² = 1, ‖long‖² = 3) the coupling is (·)^{−3}.
* **Matching Z_{h₀}.** Run their construction with the residue symbol ε³ (also a primitive quartic symbol):
  * the short node gets power 3·1 = 3, i.e. χ̄;
  * the long node gets power 3·3 = 9 ≡ 1, i.e. χ;
  * the coupling is ε^{3·(−3)} = ε^{−1}.
  That is exactly c = short root (α(c)g̃(χ̄_c)), d = long root (g̃(χ_d)), coupling χ̄_d(c). The twisting parameter m = (1, h₀).
  The FE shifts agree: the c-reflection moves the d-coordinate by 3x.
  * §2 labelled c "long". By the FE shifts c is the SHORT root; the Cartan/BD bookkeeping there swapped the root lengths.
  * The conclusions are unchanged: group W(G₂), and the c-twist α kills every polar hyperplane except the d-root one.
    Positive coroots written in α_c^∨ (long coroot) and α_d^∨ (short coroot) are α_d^∨, α_c^∨, α_c^∨+α_d^∨, α_c^∨+2α_d^∨,
    α_c^∨+3α_d^∨, 2α_c^∨+3α_d^∨. Only α_d^∨ has no α_c^∨ component.
* **What CG do not literally cover:**
  1. The archimedean twist α(c) = c/|c|, i.e. ℓ = 1 on the short node. α is not locally constant, so it is outside M(Ω).
     The Kubota FE with ℓ (DDHL (4.28)) maps ℓ ↦ −ℓ and adds (ν/|ν|)^ℓ. The orbit of the twist data under W(G₂) is finite (≤ 12).
     So the Bochner/finite-group argument should go through, but it is not written.
  2. F = Q(i) ⊉ μ₈. This is technical: the Hilbert symbol (−1, ·)₄ adds sign bookkeeping.
  3. Polynomial growth in vertical strips and polynomial dependence on the twist h₀, needed for the convexity bounds of §3.
     These are standard after Bochner, but not stated in CG.
* **Updated status of the conditional Q^{3/2}.**
  * The series behind the T-specific first moment is a CG G₂ WMDS with an archimedean twist.
  * Continuation and the W(G₂) functional equations are a theorem for the untwisted, μ₈ ⊂ F version (CG).
  * The extension to (α, Q(i)) plus convexity bounds is the remaining STANDARD-type gap. No new idea is needed for it, but it is not
    written and so not claimed as PROVED.
